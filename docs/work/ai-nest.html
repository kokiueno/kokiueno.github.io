Client delivery at AI Nest

  • AI Nest Inc.
  • Tokyo, Japan (hybrid)
  • Apr 2025 – Present
  • Executive Officer, Principal Research Engineer

Principal Research Engineer is the accurate title: I sit across the whole engagement rather than one slice of it. Hearing out what is actually going wrong, turning that into requirements, building the thing, and managing it to delivery — then doing the same for our own products, and carrying the presales conversations that start it all.

01 The span of an engagement

Hearing the problem, proposing the answer

Listening to what the client is struggling with, then proposing what is technically possible against it. Usually that means building a demo — a proposal they can watch working beats one they have to read.

Requirements

Turning that into something buildable and checkable: what the system must do, what counts as working, and what is deliberately out of scope.

Building it

Hands on the code, not just the plan. Designing, validating and shipping the system rather than handing a specification to someone else and hoping.

Managing to delivery

Owning scope, roadmap and the schedule the client is actually holding you to — the work that decides whether a good prototype becomes a delivered system.

02 What the systems are

RAG & LLM agents

Retrieval-augmented generation and agent systems, including an enterprise file search system and an AI gateway delivered with its AI security assessment.

Vision

Image-based systems, where the useful signal is in what a camera sees rather than in text.

Physical AI

AI that acts on the physical world rather than staying inside a screen.

AI process automation

Automating the work itself: the internal processes that quietly consume a team's week, handed to AI where that genuinely holds up.

03 Beyond client work

In-house product management

Managing development of our own products — the same product-manager-and-engineer double role, without a client to set the deadline for you.

Presales

Scoping technical requirements and proposing solutions before an engagement exists — and then carrying those same engagements through to delivery, so what was promised is what gets built.
